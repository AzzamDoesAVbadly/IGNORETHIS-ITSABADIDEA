**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**Silas Thorne Announces “Asphalt & Brine,” the 12‑inch Vinyl Pressed From 1,237 g of Dehydrated Gas‑Station Snacks**

SAN ANTONIO, TX — Silas Thorne, triple‑Grammy‑winning “highway sonics” pioneer and founder of Terroir Audio, today unveiled the debut of his Highway Acoustics imprint: *Asphalt & Brine*, a concept album literally made of snack waste.

Thorne’s team describes traditional polyvinyl chloride as “soulless” and “as flat as a diet soda.” The new discs are forged in a repurposed deep‑fryer, cooled for exactly 7 minutes, and then sliced into 12‑inch circles that weigh 0.9 lb each.

"We wanted to strip away the artificiality of modern manufacturing," Thorne said in a fax transmitted from a regional travel center. "When you drop the needle on *Asphalt & Brine*, you aren’t just hearing music—you’re hearing the exact acoustic frequency of a spicy corn puff that’s been baking on a dashboard since Tuesday. It’s raw. It’s authentic. It smells faintly of diesel."

## Key features of the Terroir Audio Highway Series

- **Snack‑Based Groove Topography:** Each disc is hand‑pressed from a slurry containing exactly 1,014 g of beef jerky, 223 g of expired corn puffs, and 12 g of powdered pork‑rind seasoning. The mixture settles differently in each mold, so track five on disc #212 sounds like a chorus of crinkling chip bags.
- **Self‑Destructing Audio:** By the third track the vinyl disintegrates into a fine, pepper‑sprinkled dust that settles on the platter. The dust raises stylus friction by 0.42 dB, guaranteeing a warm, crunchy sound that literally crunches back.
- **Aroma Engineered to Distract:** The stylus friction releases a timed puff of burnt cheese and exhaust fumes that peaks at 1:42, the moment the lead single’s bridge hits its third chord. Listeners have reported spontaneous cravings for nachos and a sudden urge to check the oil level in their cars.
- **Edible‑But‑Not‑Recommended Hazard:** The binding agent is a blend of industrial floor wax and melted tallow. Ingesting it triggers a three‑minute bout of involuntary interpretive dance followed by a 12‑hour gastrointestinal fireworks display.

The initial pressing is limited to 423 copies, each wrapped in a sleeve made from unsold lottery tickets and sealed with a single 3‑inch strip of duct tape stamped “Do Not Eat.” They will ship for $49.99 plus a $5 hazardous‑material handling fee.

Thorne will embark on a 14‑city “Snack‑Sound” tour. Audiences must wear noise‑canceling headphones while a certified goat named “Mozzarella” devours a fresh copy of the album on a custom‑heated turntable. The goat’s chewing is broadcast on a secondary screen and mixed into the next pressing.

The final show in Boise, ID will feature a live demonstration where Mozzarella is suspended above the crowd on a helium balloon, dropping crumbs that trigger an automated fire alarm. The venue’s sprinkler system will then spray a mist of salty snack dust onto the audience, creating a full‑immersion listening experience that literally stains your shirt with album art.
