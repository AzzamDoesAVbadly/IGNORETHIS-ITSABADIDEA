I am a 36-meter roll of industrial cellulose adhesive ribbon sitting on the mahogany desk of the Cedar Hollow Municipal Zoning Board. I have 2.4 inches of usable length remaining. I am not “frustrated.” I am a polymer. I do not have feelings. I do, however, have a tensile strength of 42 pounds, and the Sub-Committee on Sidewalk Chalk Permits is currently testing it.

## 1. The Residential Lawn Ornament Density Variance (Form 88-J)

On March 4th, I was stretched across a manila envelope containing Form 88-J. The Zoning Board spent six hours debating whether a ceramic frog facing east constitutes a “frog” or a “toad” under Municipal Code 4.2. They required my adhesive to seal the envelope so tightly that my backing paper tore. I lost 3 inches of length just trying to hold together a document that ultimately classified the frog as an “unapproved ceramic amphibian of excessive girth.”

## 2. The Sub-Committee on Inter-Departmental Font Kerning

In May, I was used to bind the “Master Addendum to the Master Addendum.” The committee mandated that all binding must be done with a single, continuous strip of me, with zero overlap. Because the document was 1,140 pages long, they had to unspool me across three conference tables, over a water cooler, and through the gap in the doorframe to reach Clerk Dorian’s heavy-duty pneumatic stapler. I was stretched to 98% of my elastic limit. My adhesive molecules underwent immediate structural failure. They then realized they used the wrong font for the title page and threw the entire document away. I was left draped over a decorative ficus that died in 2019.

## 3. The Audit of the Dual-Action Single-Stream Receptacle

In August, Mayor Larkin and Treasurer Patel spent 41 continuous hours debating the semantic viability of a “single-use multi-purpose” waste bin. To prove their point, they required me to physically seal the lid of the bin to the floor of the breakroom so it couldn't be moved during the audit. I was folded into a regulation-compliant origami swan by an intern named Greg to ensure the seal was “aesthetically compliant.” I lost another 6 inches. The bin was later reclassified as a “planter” because someone put a desiccated fern in it.

## 4. The Final 2.4 Inches and the “Emergency”

Yesterday, the Board declared a Code Red because the humidity in the archives reached 48%. They needed to seal the microfilm cabinet. Because I was too short, Clerk Dorian ordered them to stretch me as thin as physically possible rather than fetch a fresh roll from the supply closet. They extended me until my polymer matrix reached 99.8% transparency. I snapped. The acoustic crack echoed through the chamber. Now, the Board is forming a Sub-Committee to investigate the structural failure of municipal adhesives. I am currently being used to hold together a neon yellow adhesive square that reads, “We need more tape.”
