HILLSIDE, OH — At 02:13 a.m. on Monday, the silver SnackSphere Model V7 (serial 7X9‑42) near the food‑court escalator suffered a power surge that jammed 27 quarters in its coin‑return. When the jam cleared, the machine began spitting out 8 × 11 receipt‑style slips instead of chips.

The first slip read, “Ask about their favorite childhood pet before discussing finances.” Subsequent slips included, “If they laugh at your weird hobby, keep them,” and “When they mention ‘Crying in the rain,’ respond with a meme of a soggy toaster.” Each slip is printed with a unique UUID‑like code, ranging from A001‑B7 to Z999‑Q3.

"I was just trying to buy a bag of pretzels," said Mara Lenox, 29, a freelance graphic designer who works the night shift at a local co‑working space. "The machine told me to mirror body language. I tried it on my date at 7:42 p.m. and we spent the next two hours debating whether stop‑motion animation counts as a ‘real’ art form. I’ve never felt so validated by a piece of metal."

Since that night, Lenox has visited the machine three more times, each visit yielding a new tip. She now wears a tiny sticker of the SnackSphere logo on her laptop and refers to the device as “my third wheel.”

## The Rollout

Assistant manager Daryl Cho, 42, discovered a stack of 112 advice slips wedged behind the internal tray while sweeping the floor. "We ran a firmware check and found no update log for relationship content. It appears the RNG was seeded with text from an open‑source self‑help repo," he said, holding up a slip that read, “If they mention ‘pineapple on pizza,’ suggest a compromise: half pineapple, half anchovies.”

The mall has posted a neon sign above the unit: “Advice May Vary – Please Snack Responsibly.” Technicians attempted a reboot with a 9‑volt battery and a bag of Doritos, but the machine only produced another slip: “Sometimes silence is louder than a bag of chips.”

## User Testimonials

Raj Patel, 34, a high‑school chemistry teacher, shared his slip: “Send a meme that only makes sense if you’ve both watched the 1994 cartoon ‘Turbo Turtles.’” He sent the meme, his girlfriend snorted her soda, and they now name their pet rock “Crunch.” Patel notes he has inserted exactly $1.47 in exact change on each of his five visits and has yet to receive a single snack.

Lena Wu, 26, a freelance copywriter, received the advice, “When they mention a favorite book, reply with the exact page number of the first paragraph.” She used it on a blind date, correctly quoting page 23 of *Invisible Cities*, and the date now texts her a weekly “page‑number challenge.”

## Top 5 Most Actionable Tips (According to the Machine)

5. “Congratulations, you are now engaged. Select ‘yes’ for a wedding cake or ‘no’ for chips. The machine will automatically schedule a venue based on the nearest parking lot.”

## Expert Analysis

Dr. Lillian Greene, 58, professor of behavioral psychology at the fictional Midwest Institute of Human Interaction, observed, "When a vending machine becomes a pseudo‑therapist, people treat its output as low‑cost emotional labor. The advice is vague enough to be universal, yet specific enough to feel personal, which explains the rapid adoption."

Greene added that the machine’s latest slip—“You are now engaged. Please choose cake or chips”—has prompted three couples to file a joint reservation for the mall’s food‑court patio, effectively turning a snack dispenser into a wedding planner.

As of press time, the SnackSphere Model V7 continues to dispense only relationship tips. Shoppers who have tried the “silence for thirty seconds, then ask about fries” suggestion report that the ensuing quiet feels “surprisingly intimate,” though no one has yet succeeded in coaxing the machine into releasing a bag of chips.
