The HALCYON-TEK GazeLock Ring Light is a 14-inch halo of premium, anti-glare LEDs. It mounts cleanly to a desk, weighs a reassuring three pounds, and promises to "anchor your digital presence." It costs $149.

HALCYON-TEK calls this "Biometric Illumination." I call it the reason I now have to type my passwords with my eyes closed.

## What you get

Out of the box, the GazeLock features a built-in pupil-tracking sensor. The premise is simple: the light only provides full, 5000-lumen brightness when your eyes are locked directly onto the center camera lens. If your gaze drifts to the left, the right side of the ring dims. If you look down at your phone, the entire ring fades to a moody, "cinematic" 10% opacity.

For the first ten minutes, it feels like magic. You feel profoundly seen. Your cheekbones look incredible. You are the glowing center of the universe.

## The calibration period

The app requires a 14-day "Presence Streak" to unlock the "Fill Light" setting. During these two weeks, the GazeLock monitors your blink rate. If you blink for longer than 0.4 seconds, the light interprets this as "losing focus" and drops the brightness by 15% for a full minute.

I tried to read a script on my monitor while the GazeLock was running. Because my eyes naturally dart to the text, the ring light began pulsing like a dying lighthouse. My colleagues thought I was filming a documentary about a submarine in distress.

## Customer support

I contacted HALCYON-TEK to ask if there was a "Typing Mode." The support portal informed me that "Typing Mode" compromises the integrity of the GazeLock ecosystem, as true connection requires "unwavering visual devotion." It then suggested I purchase the $89 GazeLock Mechanical Keyboard, which has no keys, only braille-like bumps that you must learn by touch to avoid breaking eye contact.

I have now memorized the braille bumps. I am currently typing this review. The ring light is at 12% brightness because I just looked at my hands.

## Pros

- Excellent build quality.
- Makes your cheekbones look incredible for the 45 seconds before your eyes inevitably drift.
- Great at identifying when you are bored.

## Cons

- Requires you to unlearn the habit of looking at things.
- "Cinematic" 10% mode is actually just a dark room.
- The $89 keyless keyboard is a crime against human wrists.

## Verdict

The GazeLock Ring Light is a marvel of optical engineering trapped in a hostage situation. Two stars: one for the beautiful build quality, and one for successfully curing me of looking at my phone. I haven't seen my phone in six days. I don't know what day it is, and the light is flickering because I'm thinking about my mother.
