To the four‑member post‑hardcore act currently dropping to Drop C in my immediate vicinity: we need to talk about load distribution.

I’m the 16‑by‑8‑inch concrete masonry unit in the northwest corner, the one with a 0.3‑mm hairline fracture that runs from my top left to my bottom right. I’m also the only thing between your bass amp and a 1,372‑pound water heater that hasn’t been serviced since the last mayoral election.

I am not a drum. Stop hitting me.

## 1. You call it “raw energy.” I call it resonance frequency testing

When your drummer slams the floor tom, I don’t feel “vibes.” I feel a 42‑hertz shockwave that flexes my mortar by 0.004 mm. Last Thursday the trio from Redfield, Ohio pushed the shock to 57 dB, nudging my center of gravity three millimeters east. I spent the next eight hours holding up 1,418 pounds of drywall, a rusted fire extinguisher, and a vintage neon sign advertising “Free Pizza Tuesdays.”

## 2. The “DIY ethic” is just a fancy term for ignoring basic physics

You brag about “no corporate venues,” yet you lean three 45‑inch cabinets, each weighing 212 lb, against my cracked edge. You stack 127 unsold cassette tapes—each 0.02 lb—like a makeshift bookshelf. A guy who introduced himself only as “Moth” rested his entire 189‑lb frame on my ridge while waiting for the bathroom. I’m not an ottoman; I’m the structural spine of a building that used to house a laundromat and a taxidermy shop.

## 3. I love the music, but I also need to stay upright

When the breakdown hits and the guitars drop out, the room is quiet for exactly four seconds. In that window I can relax my compression and briefly consider a career change to decorative landscaping. Then the feedback erupts, the crowd jumps, and the joists above me start a low‑frequency groan that would make a seismologist blush. My outer layer flakes off as a fine, chalky dust that lands on your singer’s hair—consider it a free‑spun hair spray, courtesy of my degradation.

Play the next song a decibel lower, or I’ll start a new genre: “Structural Collapse Pop.”
