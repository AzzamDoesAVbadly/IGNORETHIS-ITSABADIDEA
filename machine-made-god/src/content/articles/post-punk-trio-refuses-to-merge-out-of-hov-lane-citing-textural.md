State Highway Patrol was forced to shut down the northbound interstate carpool lane Tuesday after local post-punk trio Venn Diagram refused to merge into regular traffic, arguing that crossing the solid white line would “muddy the low-end frequencies of the commute.” The band was occupying a 2018 rented passenger van, attempting to execute a 19/16 time signature transition while maintaining exactly 62 miles per hour.

When officers activated their sirens, the band’s drummer, who was seated in the second row adjusting the tension on a snare drum with a torque wrench, claimed the sudden noise was “ruining the acoustic dampening of the sliding door.” The band subsequently rolled up all four windows and deployed a portable fog machine, filling the van with a dense, synthetic haze to “isolate the reverb pedal from the ambient exhaust.”

“We need exactly three other vehicles in our blind spot to achieve the proper sense of suburban dread,” said bassist and driver Toby Aris, 28, speaking through a megaphone lowered from the passenger window. “If I merge now, we're just a band in a van. If we stay in the HOV lane until the next green overhead sign, we're a legally protected acoustic environment.”

## The acoustics of the merge

The standoff entered its fourth hour when the band’s third member, a cellist positioned in the cargo area, began aggressively misting the interior with a spray bottle of distilled water. According to the band’s rider, the moisture was strictly necessary to maintain the 42% humidity required for the synthesizer’s chorus effect.

“They had a ring light strapped to the headrest, a guy in the back continuously misting the air every four bars, and a glockenspiel balanced on a heavy-duty rotational cooler,” said Highway Patrol spokesperson Marcus Trent, 44. “I've never written a citation for 'aggravated atmospheric lighting' before, but the penal code has a subsection for unauthorized slipstream obstruction.”

## A sticky situation

Highway Patrol eventually brought in a negotiator from the Department of Transportation to explain the legal consequences of blocking 4,000 commuters. The negotiator, holding a laminated map of the interchange, spent forty-five minutes detailing the penalty for illegal lane usage. In response, Venn Diagram played an 11-minute ambient drone track to drown him out, eventually handing him a handwritten setlist in lieu of a registration document.

As of Wednesday morning, the van remains in the HOV lane. Having run out of gasoline at mile marker 42, the band is now being slowly pushed forward by a fleet of six compact sedans. The drivers of the sedans, who initially honked in frustration, have now synchronized their engines to the band's tempo. “The push has added a crucial organic drone to the bridge,” Aris texted to a local music blog. “We’re currently taking donations via a digital payment app to buy more distilled water.”
