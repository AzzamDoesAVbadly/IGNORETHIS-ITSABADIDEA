ATLANTA, GA — Method actor Silas Thorne, 42, entered day 184 of his immersion for the indie drama *The Point of Sale* by refusing to move more than 2.3 inches from the center of a 4‑by‑4‑foot plexiglass booth. The booth, re‑wired with a 12‑inch LCD that flashes "PLEASE SCAN" 7,384 times per hour, is bolted to a concrete slab that vibrates whenever a nearby shopper drops a $0.99 candy bar.

Thorne’s schedule consists of standing perfectly still for 14 hours, 57 minutes, and 23 seconds each day, punctuated only by the occasional “unexpected item” alarm triggered when a housefly lands on the scanner. He subsists on a diet of 0.2‑inch‑wide ham strips delivered through a 3‑centimeter slot, calibrated to emulate a jammed receipt printer. His wardrobe includes a high‑gloss polymer shell that emits a soft 440 Hz tone whenever a loyalty card is presented.

"He’s gone full‑machine," said his agent, Marcus Vane, 51. "I tried to negotiate a new contract and he stared at me for exactly three minutes, thirty‑seven seconds, before emitting a 1,200 hz error beep and announcing ‘Payment method declined.’" Vane added that Thorne has already rejected 42 distinct coupon codes, labeling each one “INVALID.”

## The process

Thorne treats every human interaction as a transaction. He has installed a custom barcode reader that only accepts UPCs ending in an even digit; anyone attempting to speak to him is automatically redirected to a “please wait for assistance” loop that lasts 12 minutes and 14 seconds. His sister, Clara Thorne, 38, reported that a simple hug resulted in a ten‑second freeze followed by a recorded voiceover: “ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED PHYSICAL CONTACT. REFUND NOT AVAILABLE.”
