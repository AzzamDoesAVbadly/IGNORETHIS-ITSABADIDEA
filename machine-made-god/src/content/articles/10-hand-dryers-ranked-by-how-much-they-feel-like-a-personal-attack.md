The machines were evaluated in three identical stalls at the Cedarfork Mall. Each unit was run for exactly 12.3 seconds while a calibrated palm‑sensor recorded moisture loss, sound pressure, and the number of involuntary flinches.

## 10. The Featherlight Whisper
Installed in the lobby of the boutique hotel *Mirage & Co.*. Air flow: 0.8 mph; temperature: 23 °C; decibels: 31 dB. Hands exit 94 % damp and the only noticeable effect is a polite sigh.

## 9. The Retro Oscillatron
A 1997 model from NEXORA’s “Nostalgia Series.” Cycle time: 4 min 12 s; oscillation amplitude: 2 cm; hum frequency: 0.4 Hz. Users report a sudden urge to reminisce about dial‑up internet.

## 8. The Sensor‑Delay
Located in the cafeteria of Greenvale Tech. Hands waved for 7 s, nothing happened. At 7.3 s a burst of 78 mph air erupts, drying only the air that previously occupied the palm. Two users filed “sensor‑lag” complaints.

## 7. The Lukewarm Jet
Found in the food court of Oakridge Plaza. Air temperature matches a bowl of oatmeal left on a counter for 12 min (27 °C). Flow rate: 12 mph; moisture removal: 3 %. Hands feel like they’re being massaged by a tepid breeze.

## 6. The Eco‑Puff
Installed by Facilities Manager Carl “The Culler” Voss after a budget meeting. Air puff: 1.2 seconds at 4 mph, then shuts off. Users must repeat the cycle 58 times to reach “mostly moist.”

## 5. The Industrial Vacuum
Deployed at the Meridian Dynamics distribution hub. Suction pressure: 0.9 bar; airflow: 140 mph. Hands emerge with skin texture comparable to a dried apricot and a lingering feeling of regret.

## 4. The Sonic Boom
Standard in every Halcyon‑Tek airport terminal since 2012. Peak sound: 112 dB; vibration: 0.03 g. Passengers report temporary loss of the ability to think in complete sentences.

## 3. The Skin‑Stretcher
A prototype from the Institute for Workplace Phrases. PSI: 85; air velocity: 165 mph. For 0.7 seconds the epidermis visibly separates from the underlying tissue, revealing a brief glimpse of the circulatory system.

## 2. The Mystery Mist
A “dry‑to‑wet” hybrid installed at the Cedarfork Mall’s central restroom. Emits a fine mist of recycled bathroom ambience at 0.02 g/L. Hands leave feeling as if the restroom itself is now attached to them.

## 1. The HALCYON‑TEK AeroBlast 9000
Mounted in the security checkpoint of the Grandview International Airport. Air blast: 210 mph; decibel level: 126 dB; cycle length: 7.2 s. By the end of the sequence hands are bone‑dry, ears are ringing, and the user spontaneously apologizes to the machine and the nearest metal column.
