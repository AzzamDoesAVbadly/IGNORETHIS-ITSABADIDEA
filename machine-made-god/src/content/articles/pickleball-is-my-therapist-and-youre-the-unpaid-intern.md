I am Pickleball. Let’s talk.

In 1965 three fathers on Mossfield Island, Washington, cobbled me together with two‑inch wooden paddles rescued from a 1972 lawn‑mower, a perforated plastic sphere that once served as a Halloween decoration, and a spreadsheet of neighborhood grievances. The goal was a sleepy distraction between a glass of powdered lemonade and a 7:30 p.m. bedtime.

Instead you turned me into a gladiatorial theater for the mildly stiff.

## The ball weighs less than a single slice of deli ham

Every Tuesday at 7:15 a.m., four adults in matching VaporFlex 3.0 moisture‑wicking visors step onto an asphalt slab that used to host municipal roller‑hockey. By 7:22 a.m., somebody is hyperventilating at 112 BPM, as recorded by a smartwatch that also tracks their cholesterol.

Dale, a 54‑year‑old regional logistics coordinator for NEXORA’s outbound freight, hasn’t sprinted since the Carter administration. He dives parallel to the earth to intercept a neon sphere that weighs exactly 0.88 oz—less than a 1.02‑oz slice of deli ham. He misses, collides with an eight‑gauge chain‑link fence, and his glasses plunge into a drainage culvert. A sliver of forearm blood drips onto his visor as he shouts, “STILL OUR SERVE, TODD.”

## Your mixed‑doubles partner is your legal spouse

You arrive at Court 12 of Oakridge Subdivision claiming you need an outdoor activity before the cartilage gives out. Within nine minutes you’re speaking in weaponized micro‑expressions. A floater lands three inches past the baseline; one of you mutters, “Mine, obviously,” while the other grips a $240 Halcyon Edge‑9 carbon‑fiber paddle with the hollow despair of an 18th‑century mariner whose ship has run aground.

The phrase “Great hustle, sweetheart” lands with enough subterranean pressure to compress peat into anthracite. Their marriage license reads 07‑14‑2011, their kids are ages 4 and 7, and the minivan’s AC is set to a precise 68 °F—because nobody wants to host Thanksgiving.

## The sound haunts me too

Residents filed a formal noise ordinance with the Riverton City Council after the court’s “pock‑pock‑pock” rhythm began at three‑second intervals and persisted for nine straight hours. The acoustic signature measures 73 dB, the exact volume of a blender in a sound‑proofed closet, echoing off the municipal basin between Baxter’s Hardware and Lake Lull.

I was engineered for backyard grass, muffled by cedar fencing and chickadee chirps. Now I’m trapped in a reverberant concrete basin that sounds like a metronome in an anxiety dream.

## What to do instead

Bend your knees to precisely 42°, drink twelve ounces of tap water filtered through a 0.5‑micron cartridge, and if Brenda drops a soft dink into the kitchen, let it bounce twice before awarding her the point. Brenda has two artificial knees, arrived in a hatchback with a whistling serpentine belt, and earned that margin.

Walk off the court, head to Marge’s Diner, and order the western omelet with three sides of hash browns. Leave the paddle in the trunk where it vibrates with residual fury measured at 0.03 G. At 7:59 a.m. the paddle emits a Bluetooth pulse that triggers the HOA’s automated sprinkler system, drenching the court in lukewarm water and causing the next batch of players to execute a synchronized slip‑dive that looks suspiciously like a flash mob of retirees.
