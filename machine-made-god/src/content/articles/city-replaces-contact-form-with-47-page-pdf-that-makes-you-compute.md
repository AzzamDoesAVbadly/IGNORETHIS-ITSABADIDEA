ROCHESTER, NY — The municipal website retired its one‑line "Contact Us" box on March 12 and swapped it for a 47‑page interactive PDF titled "Please Clarify Your Intent," which obliges residents to prove, via algebra, that their inquiry is not one of 14 prohibited categories.

The overhaul was mandated by the newly formed Committee on Inbound Civic Inquiries (CICI), created after the city logged exactly 14,032 emails that consisted solely of the phrase "fix it."

"The old form was a blank void that invited existential chaos," said Committee Chair Linda Aris, 58, while balancing a notarized sketch of a pothole on a ruler. "Someone once typed 'pothole' when they meant 'the meaning of municipal zoning,' and we needed a rubric that separates actionable grievances from poetic despair."

## The new protocol

1. Download the Intent Clarification Matrix (file size: 4.2 GB).  
2. On page 4, calculate the square footage of your frustration using the formula F = (π × R²) where R is the number of times you have called 311 in the past year (average: 7.3).  
3. Page 12 asks you to rank your parents' expectations on a scale of 1‑10; a rating below 5 triggers an automatic crash with the message "Insufficient emotional baseline."  
4. Page 21 demands a notarized, to‑scale diagram of the issue drawn with a 0.01 mm precision pen.  
5. Section 7 requires a 500‑word essay aligning your solution with the town’s 2018 Strategic Vision, a document that disappeared in a server migration and has not been recovered.

"I just wanted to know if the recycling center is open on Columbus Day," said resident Mark Healy, 34, after spending three hours and 27 minutes attempting to bypass Section 3. "The PDF told me to rank my parents' expectations, I gave them a 4, and it crashed, citing 'insufficient emotional baseline.'"

## Friction as a feature

Dr. Elias Penhaligon, senior fellow at the Institute for Digital Friction, reported that the new system has reduced the city's workload to zero because nobody can successfully submit a complaint.

"Since implementing the matrix, the town hasn't filled a single pothole, but satisfaction scores are up 12.4 % because the complaint pipeline is effectively a black hole," Dr. Penhaligon said while adjusting his monocle to the exact 2.7 mm gap required by the PDF's digital signature protocol.

The PDF’s 4.2‑gigabyte size also triggers a firewall cascade that shuts down the public library’s Wi‑Fi and activates the city’s fire alarm every time someone clicks "Download."

Feedback is collected on page 46, which itself requires a digital notarization from a notary who has already completed the matrix. As a result, the committee has received zero responses.

To discuss the "low response rate," the committee scheduled a town hall for next Tuesday. Attendance requires submitting a "Request to Attend" form, which is currently stuck on a CAPTCHA that asks users to identify the emotional state of a 1998‑era dial‑up modem.
