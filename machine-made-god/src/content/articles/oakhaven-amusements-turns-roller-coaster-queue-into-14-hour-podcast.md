OAKHAVEN, OH — The Hyperion roller‑coaster line, normally a 0.68‑mile serpentine of 400 guests, has been repurposed as a 14‑hour, 12‑track audio experience titled *The Leaky Roof Chronicles*.

The park’s “Narrative Immersion Pathway” was marketed as a way to shave 23% off perceived wait time by replacing idle chatter with a “compelling, slow‑burn story.” In practice it replaces the scream of steel with the sound of a facilities manager, Greg Harlow, counting drywall screws while he searches for three tarp quotes.

“I’ve been here since 6 a.m., and Greg still hasn’t called the guy with the unlicensed tarp,” said Brenda Vance, 54, a retired teacher from Fort Wayne, adjusting her 73‑piece sun‑hat equipped with a solar‑powered fan. “The $400 quote is probably shoddy, but I can’t leave until Greg finally realizes he can’t afford it.”

## The Tarp

Park literature advertises the Hyperion ride as a “90‑minute plunge into the stratosphere.” The pre‑ride audio, however, devotes 14 hours to Greg’s escalating anxiety over a ceiling tile that refuses to stay put. At hour 2, Greg discovers the drip is actually condensation from the HVAC unit. At hour 5, a six‑minute monologue from the insurance adjuster’s cat, Mr. Whiskers, dominates the soundtrack. By hour 7, Greg files an insurance claim only to learn his deductible exceeds the cost of a new roof. Hour 9 finds him in his 2008 Honda Civic, staring at a spreadsheet titled “Solo Lunch Expense” while a background track of ticking clocks plays on repeat.

“We wanted the queue to feel like a high‑stakes drama,” said Todd Linsey, 42, Senior Narrative Architect for Oakhaven Amusements. “We tested the audio on 12,453 focus‑group participants; 99.7% reported they were ‘still waiting for the next plot twist.’"

Linsey added that the audio is not looped. Guests who abandon the line must restart at the very first water stain, which lasts 3 seconds and is accompanied by a recorded sigh from the breakroom thermostat.

## A Growing Backlog

Behavioral economist Dr. Aris Thorne of the Institute for Leisure Friction noted that the saga mirrors the experience of waiting on hold for a customer‑service representative. “The brain seeks closure, even when the closure is a man named Craig finally agreeing to patch a vent with the right sealant,” Thorne said. “We measured a 7.4% cortisol spike and a 12% increase in pretzel consumption.”

As the audio drags on, the physical line has spilled into the adjacent petting zoo, now housing 67 llamas, 102 toddlers, and an impromptu goat‑herding competition. Park staff hand out complimentary pretzels flavored “paper‑clip” to anyone who reaches the “Greg’s Honda Civic” segment.

## Status

At 2:15 p.m., officials announced the Hyperion coaster would remain closed for the day due to a misaligned loading gate that resembles a shrug. Rather than disperse, the 400 guests in the queue took a synchronized step forward, eager to hear whether Greg finally submits his expense report.

The climax arrived at hour 14:03, when a delivery truck pulled up bearing a tarp made entirely of recycled Instagram stories. The contractor, a unicycling freelancer named “Tarp‑Man,” alighted, announced, “I’m here to cover the leak—and your existential dread,” before tossing the tarp into the air, where it caught the sun and reflected a perfect “You’re still waiting” meme for the final 12 seconds of the podcast.
