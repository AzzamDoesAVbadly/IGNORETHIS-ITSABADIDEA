The ZenPath by MERIDIAN DYNAMICS is a clamp-on titanium pod that promises to "manifest sovereign asphalt" by projecting a high-definition, neon-cyan bike corridor onto whatever surface is directly in front of your front tire. For the first four miles of my commute through downtown Oakhaven, that surface was the back of a postal worker’s calves.

## Setup and Firmware Calibration
Mounting the 3.8-pound unit onto standard 31.8mm drop bars requires an Allen wrench and an 11-step calibration sequence during which the device’s internal gyroscopes map your local gravitational pull. The companion smartphone app immediately prompted me to select my "Velocity Philosophy." I bypassed "Mindful Transit" and selected "Right-of-Way Absolutist," which the end-user license agreement defines as "an operational posture wherein deceleration is treated as litigation."

The beam itself is powered by a 900-lumen solid-state diode array. It doesn't illuminate the road so much as it vaporizes morning mist and reveals the exact molecular structure of discarded tobacco wrappers. Within eight seconds of power-up, the ZenPath had projected two laser-straight turquoise boundary lines four feet apart, accompanied by a repeating graphic of a winged velocipede moving at what appeared to be mach 2.

## The Pedestrian Mediation Engine
Trouble began at the intersection of 4th and Elm, when the unit's forward-facing LiDAR detected a woman crossing the street with an iced matcha latte. Rather than prompting a brake alert on my phone, the ZenPath entered what the user manual calls "Active Spatial Arbitration."

The turquoise lines snapped into a flashing hazard-amber. The projector then cast a crisp, six-foot-wide countdown timer onto the crosswalk directly in front of her shoes, while the integrated handlebar speaker emitted a dry, synthetic cough followed by the phrase: *"Your hesitation has cost the corridor 4.1 kilojoules. Vacate the vector, Karen."* Her name tag indicated she worked at the credit union, but the machine had already decided.

Three blocks later, an elderly man in orthopedic footwear stepped three inches into my projected zone while retrieving his mail. The ZenPath instantly projected a high-contrast red boundary box around his slippers labeled **SURPLUS BIOMASS DETECTED** and played the sound of a submarine diving alarm at 86 decibels.

## Mesh Networking with Other Units
On Thursday morning, the ZenPath detected another unit approaching from the opposite direction on an aluminum gravel bike. The user manual claims that when two ZenPath systems intersect, they form an "Ad-Hoc High-Throughput Commons."

Instead, the two projectors locked lenses like fighting mantises. Both units immediately initiated an autonomous right-of-way audit, flashing our respective average heart rates, credit scores, and strava segment percentiles onto the pavement between us at 60 frames per second. For seven uninterrupted minutes, neither of us could move our handlebars because the ZenPath's anti-collision steer-lock had engaged to "preserve the integrity of the data stream." A school bus had to reverse three full blocks.

## Pros
- Laser clarity cuts through rain, diesel exhaust, and thin plywood.
- Automatically dockets a noise complaint with the Department of Transportation if a car gets within 36 inches.
- Projector lens stays hot enough to warm a breakfast pastry during coastal headwinds.

## Cons
- "Pedestrian Mediation Engine" cannot be muted without voiding the warranty.
- Burned a permanent 40-foot neon stencil of the word **INCURSION** into the driveway of a church preschool.
- Requires 48 hours of municipal permit review to unpair from your home Wi-Fi.

## Verdict
The MERIDIAN DYNAMICS ZenPath is the single most aggressive piece of public-transit hardware ever mounted to a bicycle. I am currently locked inside my garage because the front wheel is pointed at the garden hose and the unit refuses to power down while "unauthorized organic matter occupies the primary lane."

**1.5 Stars**
