EASTWOOD, IN — Twelve members of the Eastwood Public Library’s "Mystery & Mayhem" book club were liberated from a commercial escape room Tuesday morning after a 19-hour ordeal involving mandatory sensitivity training, three simulated layoffs, and a grueling audit of their leisure time.

The group entered the "Corporate Conundrum" experience at 7:13 p.m., operated by team-building firm Synergetic Solutions. While the room began as a standard puzzle, the exit door locked permanently at 8:02 p.m. when the group failed to properly format a simulated expense report for a trip to a fictional trade show in Omaha.

"We thought the 'Performance Review' puzzle was just a theme," said Megan Harlow, 34, who spent six hours attempting to justify her existence to a wall-mounted tablet. "But then the room dimmed, a recording of a disappointed middle manager played over the speakers, and we were told that until our 'deliverables' met the company's 2024 strategic vision, we were technically in a 'performance improvement plan' and could not leave the premises."

By midnight, the room's difficulty shifted from puzzles to bureaucracy. To unlock the restroom, the group was required to submit a formal 'Request for Biological Leave' form in triplicate, which was then rejected by an automated system because they used the wrong shade of blue ink.

At 3:14 a.m., the experience entered the "Restructuring Phase." A voice identified as "Regional Director Vance" announced that three members of the book club had been "downsized" for lack of synergy. These individuals were forced to stand in a designated 'Redundant Zone'—a 4x4 square of gray carpet—where they were prohibited from speaking to their former colleagues unless they first sent a calendar invite for a "touch-base" meeting.

By dawn, the group had devolved into a desperate corporate hierarchy. Jared Kline, 27, was appointed 'Interim Chief Compliance Officer' and began issuing mandatory 15-minute 'wellness bursts' consisting of staring at a picture of a pebble while listening to a recording of a corporate lawyer reading the terms and conditions of a software license.

The ordeal reached its peak at 11:00 a.m. Wednesday, when the group was informed that the exit door would only open once they successfully navigated a "Cross-Functional Synergy Conflict." This required the club to rewrite the plot of their current read, *The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo*, as a series of five high-impact PowerPoint slides focusing on 'Optimizing Forensic Resource Allocation' and 'Stakeholder Management in the Private Investigator Sector.'

Rescue crews arrived at 2:13 p.m. to find the group huddled in the corner, meticulously drafting a 40-page quarterly projection for a fictitious logistics company called 'OmniFreight,' using only a single shared stapler and a box of expired paperclips.
