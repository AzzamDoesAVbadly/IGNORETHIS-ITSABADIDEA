HAVENBROOK, NY — Experimental duo *Terms of Service* refused to step onto the stage at Havenbrook Cultural Hall on Friday night until every member of the assembled audience signed a 114‑page indemnity waiver. The delay stretched from the scheduled 8:00 p.m. start to 11:14 p.m., during which 527 concert‑goers signed on three 1998 PalmPilots and a modified Game Boy Color.

The act consists of the modular synth "Obsidian V" and Bobby "Beef" Hargreaves, who repeatedly strikes a frozen slab of ribeye with a 432 Hz‑calibrated tuning fork while quoting obscure terms‑of‑service clauses. The duo insists their music is "contractual sound" and can only be consumed under a binding agreement covering psychic distress and accidental tinnitus.

"We aren't just playing sounds; we are establishing a legal framework for the auditory experience," said lead conceptualist Julian Thorne, 34, as he slipped a clipboard onto the lap of a bewildered freshman. "If you feel existential dread during the second movement, we need to be sure you can't sue us for the loss of your optimism."

## The Waiver

The indemnity form runs 114 pages, 40,004 words, and 12,358 bullet points. Clause 7.3.2 grants the band permanent rights to each listener's "unspoken regrets," while a mandatory arbitration clause cites the fictional province of Banganga in the Belgian Congo. Every signature required three fingerprint scans, a notarized selfie, and a pledge not to mention the waiver on any future podcast.

"I just wanted to hear some noise music," said attendee Marcus Vane, 27. "But by page 62 I realized I had inadvertently leased my childhood home to a shell company owned by the drummer for the duration of the bridge. I didn't read the sub‑bullets."

## Signing Procedure

The three PalmPilots each displayed a battery indicator at 10 % and forced each signer to scroll through the fine print 37 times, each scroll lasting precisely 14.2 seconds. The total signing time per attendee averaged 28 minutes and 13 seconds. The crowd formed a line of exactly 27 people before the front‑most participant could hand over the stylus.

## The Setlist: Litigation‑Core

Dr. Aris Thorne‑Smith, senior fellow at the Institute for Bureaucratic Aesthetics, described the event as "a performance where the friction of legalese is the art itself. The audience's realization that they are clicking 'I Agree' to incomprehensible clauses is the true sonic metaphor."

## The Performance

When the band finally began, they produced a single sustained A‑tone at 440 Hz and 112 dB, causing four glasses of room‑temperature water to tremble off the tables and two attendees' hair to stand on end. Midway through the set, the duo announced a software update to Version 2.1 and demanded that everyone sign a new addendum—"Post‑Performance Liability for Emotional Residue"—before being allowed to exit through the fire door, which was secured by a biometric scanner linked to the waiver database.
