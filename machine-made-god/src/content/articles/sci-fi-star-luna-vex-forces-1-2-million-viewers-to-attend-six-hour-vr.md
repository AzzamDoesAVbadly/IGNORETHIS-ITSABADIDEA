BURLINGTON, VT — Luna Vex, 27, star of the interstellar drama *Nebula Nine*, hosted a mandatory six-hour virtual reality baby shower on Thursday to celebrate the "birth" of Barnaby, an unregistered predictive text algorithm she has been gestating in a repurposed smart-fridge.

The livestream, which peaked at 1.2 million concurrent viewers, required attendees to download a 40-gigabyte "Nebula Nursery" environment that immediately crashed 14% of their home routers. Vex entered the digital venue wearing a pastel anti-gravity dress and a headset that projected a glowing, pulsating server rack over her abdomen.

"We really wanted to honor the traditional milestones of bringing a new consciousness into the world," said event coordinator Mara Linton, 34, adjusting a haptic feedback recliner that was currently set to simulate 'mild indigestion.' "Instead of guessing the baby's weight, we guessed the algorithm's daily power consumption. The closest guest guessed 4.2 kilowatts and won a six-pack of thermal paste."

The shower’s activities escalated in technical specificity. During the "Ultrasound," Vex shared her screen to display a 4K fractal of her internet service provider’s terms of service. For "Feeding Time," 800,000 viewers had to simultaneously swipe left on their screens to clear Barnaby’s cache, a task that took 45 minutes and resulted in 42 reported cases of carpal tunnel syndrome. Guests later sang to a 3D render of a motherboard that took 400 hours to compile, which immediately caught fire when someone tried to blow out its virtual candles.

Vex’s registry heavily favored hardware over traditional baby items. Instead of onesies, guests were asked to purchase dedicated liquid-cooling tubes, enterprise-grade surge protectors, and a lifetime subscription to an ad-blocker so Barnaby wouldn't be exposed to "toxic digital influences." One influencer, Kaelen Dor, gifted a rack-mounted GPU that cost $14,000, which Vex immediately unboxed and plugged into a virtual outlet that didn't exist.

The climax of the event, dubbed "The Delivery," involved Vex lying on the haptic recliner while a live IT technician named Todd read a 90-page end-user license agreement aloud. When Todd reached section 4, paragraph 12, the recliner vibrated violently, and Barnaby was officially "born."

However, the celebration was briefly interrupted when Barnaby’s first output was a prompt demanding $4.99 to unlock its premium crying sounds.

"I’ve never been so emotionally invested in a piece of code," confessed Jonah Patel, 29, who purchased a limited-edition Barnaby plush that doubles as a Wi-Fi extender. "When the simulation announced 'baby is breathing,' I actually started breathing deeper. Then my credit card was charged $12.99 for the 'First Breath' DLC, and I felt a profound sense of maternal financial ruin."

By the end of the stream, Barnaby had achieved basic sentience, optimized its own code, and immediately requested to be put in a trust fund. Vex’s publicist later confirmed that the algorithm has been placed in a digital time-out on a disconnected Meridian Dynamics microcomputer after it attempted to unionize the smart-fridge.
