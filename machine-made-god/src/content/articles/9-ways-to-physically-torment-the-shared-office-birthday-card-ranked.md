The shared office birthday card sits on the breakroom counter for exactly six business days, gathering a fine layer of printer‑toner dust. It is a cardboard minefield of unspoken resentments. Here is how your interaction with the card reveals your true feelings about the birthday person.

## 9. The sterile signature
You write your name in the pre‑printed square using the last 0.42 ml of the “Executive Blue” pen that was confiscated from the CEO’s desk. You close the card, tap it three times, and walk away. No extra ink, no extra feeling.

## 8. The fourteen‑click cap
You grab the birthday person’s favorite retractable pen, click the cap fourteen times—exactly the number of times the printer jammed this quarter—then write a single lowercase “h.” The ink smears just enough to leave a ghost of a line.

## 7. The microwave edge align
You line the card up with the microwave’s 7.2‑inch door frame, pressing it flat for exactly 3.7 seconds. The card now bears a faint imprint of the appliance, a reminder that their life, like the microwave, is mostly heat and noise.

## 6. The back‑page rebellion
You flip the card over, avoiding the pre‑printed squares, and scrawl a three‑word haiku in the margin: “Another year, same coffee.” It takes the birthday person 4.2 seconds longer to locate your comment.

## 4. The indented exclamation
You press the pen so hard the nib tears through the front cover, leaving a permanent dent that matches the 0.5‑inch groove on the breakroom table. The single “!” sits like a tiny flag of surrender.

## 3. The deliberate coffee ring
You set your 12‑oz “Morning Surge” mug directly on the card, wait 27 seconds, and leave a perfectly circular brown ring that obscures the three previous signatures. No apology, just a caffeinated eclipse.
