You are tired. You are carrying three plastic bags of overpriced lobby snacks, including a family-sized bag of pretzels you fully intend to eat in bed. You just need to get to room 412. But the little black rectangle in your hand has other plans. Here are the ways it can deny you, ranked from a minor technical glitch to a profound moral reckoning.

## 10. The standard red flash

A clean, silent rejection. The lock doesn't hate you; it just doesn't know you. This is usually caused by your phone keys resting too close in your pocket, or the sheer ambient static of your own disappointment.

## 9. The delayed red flash

It thinks about it for a full two seconds before rejecting you. This implies it recognized your key, considered your request, and actively chose to say no because it knows you forgot to tip the bellhop.

## 8. The "Wrong Room" denial

You are standing in front of room 412. The card insists you are not. You are now forced to question the fundamental nature of room numbers, your own spatial awareness, and whether you are actually in a simulation.

## 7. The "Please See Front Desk" denial

The ultimate bureaucratic pass. The card is not just denying you entry; it is giving you homework. You now have to walk back to the lobby in your socks to beg for a new one, passing the very bellhop you failed to tip.

## 6. The intermittent success

It works on the first try, but when you return from the ice machine, it rejects you. It is establishing boundaries. It wants to see how much you need it, and it wants you to stand in the hallway wondering if you have lost your edge.

## 5. The physical resistance

The light turns green, but the deadbolt physically refuses to retract. The lock is engaging in a silent, mechanical protest against your very presence, holding the metal firm out of pure spite.

## 4. The realization you are holding a grocery loyalty card

You have been aggressively swiping a piece of plastic that only offers twelve cents off gummy vitamins. The lock didn't deny you entry. You just weren't holding a key, and now you have to go back to the deli to get your actual room key.

## 3. The "Room Does Not Exist" error

You swipe. The digital screen reads: "Invalid Room Number." You look at the brass plaque. It says 412. The hotel is gaslighting you, and the building's architecture is in on it. You will eventually just sleep in the hallway.

## 2. The "Checkout Time Passed" error, applied at 2:00 PM

The card reminds you that your reservation ended four hours ago. It is no longer a locking mechanism; it is a formal eviction notice delivered via radio frequency. You are now a trespasser in your own temporary life.

## 1. The green light, the click, and the printed receipt

The door unlocks, but a small thermal printer mounted inside the lock dispenses a slip of paper that reads: "We saw you take the shampoo. Go back downstairs."
