BOULDER, CO — At 09:13 a.m. on Saturday, 26 employees of Meridian Dynamics were ushered into the abandoned Self‑Scan Plaza on Main Street for a “self‑sustaining” team‑building exercise. After exactly 4 hours, 17 minutes, and 42 seconds, three coffee mugs, two security badges, and the ID badge of senior analyst Tori McClain (who now shows up in the system as *OUT‑OF‑STOCK*) were unaccounted for.

## The exercise

The Wellness Committee advertised the day as a “hands‑on trust drill” requiring participants to navigate twelve active scanners, each pre‑loaded with 342 distinct product barcodes ranging from “Organic Kale” (SKU #K‑001) to “Synthetic Motivation” (SKU #M‑999). Every scan was accompanied by a mandatory recitation of the company mission: “We automate, we iterate, we dominate.”

"I thought we were supposed to scan groceries, not ourselves," complained Jenna Kline, 32, junior analyst, when her scanner flashed *ERR‑SELF‑SCAN* for the third time and asked her to confirm her date of birth, favorite childhood cartoon, and the exact number of beans in a standard espresso shot.

Marcus Velasquez, 45, senior project manager, reported that the scavenger‑hunt portion required finding a *lost* coffee mug hidden in aisle 7. He found the mug in a recycling bin, but the coffee had evaporated and the mug’s label now read “Property of the Collective Unconscious.”

The machines, supplied by the now‑defunct vendor Greeley Gadgets, emitted a chime that grew, by 3 dB per hour, indistinguishable from a funeral toll. At 12:06 p.m., scanner #7 announced “Item not found: Motivation,” prompting a collective sigh.

## The missing inventory

At 2:41 p.m., two security badges disappeared from the staff locker. An internal audit logged them under SKU *BADGE‑001* with a price of $0.00, weight 0.0 g, and a tax code of “Existential Crisis.”

The most puzzling loss was Tori McClain’s badge, which was scanned as “Human Labor” and now appears on the inventory list as:

> 1 × Human Labor – $0.00 – Tax: Existential Crisis – Status: Out of Stock

When asked for comment, HR coordinator Lila Huang, 28, held a clipboard stamped “All Scans Are Final” and replied, “We’re still waiting for the receipt to print.”

## Corporate response

Meridian Dynamics released a statement claiming the retreat “successfully increased employee awareness of automated processes and reinforced personal accountability.” The statement promised a follow‑up survey, which remains locked behind a password‑protected scanner that demands a 12‑digit code derived from the employee’s last performance review.

At press time, a lone scanner at the exit continued to beep every 7 seconds, awaiting a barcode that never arrives. The overhead lights flickered in a pattern that matches the Morse code for “HELP.”

Management has already booked a second retreat for Q3 at a decommissioned password‑reset kiosk, where participants will be asked to “reset their expectations.”
