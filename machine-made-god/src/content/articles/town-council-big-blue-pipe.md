MILLBROOK FALLS, PA — In a historic session that ran nearly four hours, the Millbrook Falls Town Council voted 7–2 Tuesday night to formally stop pretending anyone knows what the big blue pipe behind the public library does.

The pipe, roughly 18 inches in diameter and painted a shade the town's records describe as "municipal blue," emerges from the ground behind the library, curves upward to a height of about four feet, and then descends back into the ground about six feet away. It has been on every council agenda since 1986, usually under "Old Business."

"For thirty-eight years we've been nodding at that pipe like we understood it," said Councilmember Rhonda Vickers, who introduced the resolution. "Every year someone asks, and every year someone says 'that's the water thing' or 'that's for the drainage' with total confidence. Tonight, we stop nodding."

## A long history of confidence

According to meeting minutes, the pipe has been identified at various times as "the water thing," "the gas thing," "part of the old system," "the new system," "something to do with the bridge," and, in 1994, "a decorative element."

In 2003, the town spent $11,000 on a study of the pipe. The study's conclusion, delivered in a 140-page binder, reads in full: "The pipe appears to be functioning."

Public works director Stan Ochoa, who has held the job for 19 years, testified that he has "walked past it thousands of times" and has "always assumed someone else knew."

"I thought the previous director knew," Ochoa said. "He told me he thought the director before him knew. That man has passed away. I'd like to believe he knew. I'd like to believe that very much."

## The dissent

The two dissenting votes came from Councilmembers Earl Dobbins and Patricia Haynes, who argued that admitting nobody knows what the pipe does could "undermine public confidence in infrastructure generally."

"If people find out we don't know what the blue pipe does," Haynes said, "they're going to start asking about the green box by the elementary school. And frankly, nobody is ready for that conversation."

## What happens now

Under the resolution, the pipe will be reclassified in town records from "Utility, Active" to "Pipe, Blue." A small plaque will be installed reading: "We don't know. Please don't touch it."

Residents interviewed after the meeting were largely supportive. "It's honestly a relief," said one. "I've been lying to my kids about it for years. I told them it was where the library breathes."
