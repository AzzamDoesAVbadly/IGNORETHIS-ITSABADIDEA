CHICAGO — Global pop sensation Cassian Vane paused his 'Neon Solitude' arena tour on Tuesday night to personally reconfigure the smartphone notification settings of the first 800 attendees, halting the show after a single factory default text alert 'shattered the spatial audio.'

Vane, 29, stopped mid-chorus of 'Glass Lungs' when a fan in row four received an automated pharmacy reminder using the default 'Urgent Siren' tone. According to Vane’s audio team, the three-note chime directly clashed with the F-sharp minor key of the bridge, causing the venue's subwoofers to physically reject the melody.

Instead of asking the crowd to silence their devices, Vane demanded a full settings audit. Security footage showed the pop star pacing the general admission pit with a glowing slate, forcing fans to navigate their primary audio menus while he watched over their shoulders.

'I was just trying to find the text tone dropdown while he stood three inches from my face,' said Marnie Hodges, 28, a dental hygienist in row three. 'He kept sighing every time I accidentally opened the wallpaper menu. At one point, he saw my daily screen time was four hours and asked me if I was okay at home. I told him I was watching a documentary about cheese, and he made me change my ringtone to Single Drip as punishment.'

By the 30-minute mark, Vane’s sonic archivist, Declan Rourke, had to deploy a team of roadies with cotton swabs to clean the charging ports of fans whose devices were deemed 'acoustically sluggish.' Rourke confirmed that three attendees were forced to factory-reset their phones after their default alarm tone was discovered to be a 2014 marimba track. 'It doesn't just interrupt the song,' Rourke said, adjusting a pair of noise-canceling headphones. 'It actively un-sings it. We had to extract the marimba from the venue's reverb tail using a digital spatula.'

The settings overhaul was finally completed at 10:15 p.m., and Vane resumed the concert to a perfectly silent, highly optimized crowd. However, during the encore, the show was halted a second time after Vane discovered that 14 fans in section 104 had their home screen widgets set to the local weather module, which he claimed was 'bringing a draft into the venue.' Vane then spent 45 minutes manually changing their home screens to a high-resolution image of his own left clavicle before allowing the band to strike the final chord.
