**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**MERIDIAN DYNAMICS Launches "Curated Exit Corridor," a Next-Generation Museum Gift Shop Where The Architecture Actively Prevents Patrons From Leaving**

CHICAGO, IL — MERIDIAN DYNAMICS, a global leader in experiential retail, today announced the rollout of the Curated Exit Corridor, a 4,000-square-foot museum gift shop where the physical layout actively prevents patrons from reaching the cash register.

Unlike traditional museum shops that allow visitors to purchase a single canvas tote and walk out into natural sunlight, the Curated Exit Corridor utilizes a series of motorized velvet ropes and shifting drywall partitions. The space is optimized to unveil a new visual "discovery" every 4.7 seconds, ensuring patrons remain physically wedged between the overpriced ceramic mugs and the structural support pillars. Patrons attempting to locate the front door will find themselves funneled into a 40-foot labyrinth of locally sourced beeswax and "vintage-inspired" enamel pins.

"We noticed that museum visitors were spending an average of four minutes in the gift shop before returning to their vehicles," said MERIDIAN DYNAMICS Chief Experience Officer Aris Thorne. "With this new layout, we’ve extended that dwell time to an estimated six to eight business days. We found that if you make the floor slightly sticky every twelve feet, people just assume they're having a nostalgic moment and keep browsing."

**Key features of the Curated Exit Corridor:**

- **Kinematic Merchandising:** High-margin items like artisanal coasters are mounted on pneumatic pistons. If a patron reaches for a coaster, the shelf instantly drops three inches, forcing the patron to squat deeper and experience the dopamine hit of physical exertion.
- **Receding Checkout:** The cash register is mounted on a hidden track. If a patron walks toward it at a normal pace, the counter moves away at exactly 1.4 miles per hour. Patrons must break into a light jog to complete a transaction, which automatically adds a 15% "athleticism surcharge" to their total.
- **Variable-Ratio Pricing:** The price of the museum-branded wool socks fluctuates dynamically based on the patron's pupil dilation. If a patron looks away for more than three seconds, the price increases by $4. If they blink, the socks are discontinued.
- **Perpetual Resolution Displays:** Physical displays require the user to align a wooden token with a painted shadow on a pedestal. Once aligned, a hidden employee immediately knocks the token over with a broom handle to maintain a state of perpetual, unresolved completion.
- **Circadian Disruption Lighting:** The ambient lighting transitions from warm gallery glow to the exact harsh, blue-light frequency of a smartphone screen at 3:00 AM. This is paired with a subsonic hum that plays a slowed-down, 14-hour audio loop of a cashier named Brenda asking if you found everything okay.

**Context and Rollout**

The first Curated Exit Corridor will open next month at the Regional Center for Mid-Century Textiles. Patrons are advised to bring water, a sleeping bag, and a signed waiver, as the ambient lighting is calibrated to permanently disrupt circadian rhythms in favor of "just one more look." Early beta testing showed a 400% increase in unpurchased tote bag contemplation, and three patrons developed a symbiotic relationship with a display of decorative gourds.

"We've also installed a misting system that sprays a fine aerosol of lavender and regret every time someone looks at their watch," Thorne added, before pausing to adjust a display of brass candle snuffers. "Currently, our longest-staying patron has been in the sock section since Tuesday. We had to start sliding plates of baked ziti under the display case to keep him alive, and he's now the regional manager of hosiery."
