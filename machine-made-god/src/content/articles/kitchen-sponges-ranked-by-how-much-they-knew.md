Every sponge enters your home bright, firm and innocent. Every sponge leaves it gray, soft and changed. In between, it learns things about you that nobody else knows. We've ranked them from least to most aware.

## 8. The one you threw out after two days

Barely got started. Saw one pasta pot and one spilled smoothie. Left with the clean conscience of a sponge that never saw you eat cereal over the sink at midnight. It knew nothing. Envy it.

## 7. The decorative sponge shaped like a fruit

Bought because it was cute. Never used, because it was cute. Sat on the windowsill for nine months watching the regular sponges do the real work, developing the specific resentment of a guest who was never asked to help.

## 6. The dish wand

Kept you at arm's length. Never touched your hands. Cleaned your mugs with the detached professionalism of a contractor. Knew what you ate, but not how you felt about it.

## 5. The scrubby-side sponge you used on a nonstick pan once

Knows it did something wrong. Knows you know. Neither of you has brought it up. The pan has never been the same, and every time it's used, both of them think about it.

## 4. The sponge from your first apartment

Saw you learn to cook, badly. Was there for the smoke alarm, the rice that was both burnt and raw, and the night you cried over a lasagna. It absorbed some of that. It still had a few crumbs of that lasagna when you moved out. You left it behind like a witness you couldn't afford to bring.

## 3. The sponge that went through the microwave "to sanitize it"

Survived. Was never the same. Now has a slight scorched smell and a thousand-yard stare. Understands that the people who love you can also put you in a microwave for two minutes on high, which is more than most sponges ever learn.

## 2. The sponge you kept too long

You know the one. It wasn't a sponge anymore so much as a damp archive. It knew about the three-week period where you only ate tortilla chips. It knew the name of your ex because it heard you say it while aggressively scrubbing a skillet. When you finally threw it away it did not go quietly. It left a smell. That was its statement.

## 1. The sponge currently by your sink

It is watching you read this. It knows you're avoiding the dishes by reading a list about sponges. It has seen the pan you've been "letting soak" since Sunday. It is not angry. It is patient. It has all the time in the world, and so, it believes, do you.
