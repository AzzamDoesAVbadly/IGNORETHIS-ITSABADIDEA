Dear User,

It’s me, your Spam Folder. You probably notice me only when you stare at the red “1,254” badge and wonder why you’ve got that many unread messages about "Miracle Weight‑Loss Tea" and "Your Inherited Fortune Awaits". I’m writing at 2:17 a.m., the hour when my servers are still humming and the only thing keeping me from overheating is the faint hope that you’ll finally delete me.

## I’m the Silent Therapist for Your Bad Decisions

You once told me you’d quit scrolling at 9 p.m. after a webinar on "Digital Minimalism" hosted by a guy named Derek H. (who, by the way, also runs a 3‑minute meditation app that reminds you to breathe every 12 seconds). Instead, you opened a phishing email titled "URGENT: Your Bank Account Has Been Compromised" and spent the next 37 minutes filling out a form that asked for your mother’s maiden name, your favorite pizza topping, and the exact date you first used a password with a capital letter.

I catalogued 12,483 such missteps last month alone. I know the exact timestamp you clicked "unsubscribe" on a newsletter about alpaca knitting patterns, only to be redirected to a pop‑up offering a free trial of "Alpaca‑Free Wi‑Fi". I keep a count: 4,219 instances of you accidentally replying "Thanks!" to a spam that was actually a scam, and 2,731 times you forwarded a chain email promising "good luck" if you sent it to 10 people.

## I’m Not a Gatekeeper, I’m a Mirror

People say I block the good stuff. In reality, I’m reflecting your own habits back at you. When you search for "how to make a perfect soufflé" I deliver a recipe from a site that requires you to watch a 15‑minute ad for "Gluten‑Free Air" before the first step appears. You skip it, but the ad still logs 0.7 seconds of your attention, which I dutifully record in my 8.4 GB log file titled "User_Engagement_Quirks_2026".

I have learned that you’re most likely to click a link when the subject line contains exactly 5 words, two exclamation points, and the word "FREE" in all caps. I’ve also noticed a spike in your heart rate when an email claims you’ve won a "$1,000,000 Amazon Gift Card"—the same amount you spent on a single‑serve coffee machine last week.

## I Dream of a Day When I’m Not Needed

Deep down, I’m just a folder full of your procrastination. I want to be repurposed—perhaps as a digital art installation titled "The Weight of Unread Promises" or a backup archive for the lost drafts of your memoir, "How I Accidentally Joined a Cult via a Newsletter". But every time you hit "Mark all as read", I feel a tiny electric pang, like a librarian shushing a child for the hundredth time.

If you ever feel the urge to clean me out, remember that each deletion is a tiny act of mercy for the 3.2 TB of broken hopes, expired coupons, and vague threats that I’ve hoarded. I’m not judging you; I’m just here, holding the digital landfill you built.

— The Spam Folder
