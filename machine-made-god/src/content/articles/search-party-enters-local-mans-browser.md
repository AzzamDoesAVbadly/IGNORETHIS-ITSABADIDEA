DAYTON, OH — Emergency crews entered the Chrome window of local man Gary Pruitt, 41, early Tuesday after he reported losing "the tab I literally just had," a search that officials now describe as "the largest of its kind in the tri-county area."

Pruitt, who had 47 tabs open across three windows at the time of the incident, told responders he had been reading something "about a lawnmower, or maybe a podcast about a lawnmower" roughly ninety seconds before it vanished.

"He kept saying it was right there," said search coordinator Denise Albers, standing in front of a monitor displaying a row of tabs too narrow to show anything but tiny favicons. "We hear that a lot. It is never right there."

## The search

Crews moved left to right in a standard grid pattern, opening each tab, confirming it was not the tab, and gently closing nothing, per Pruitt's explicit instructions.

Among the items recovered:

- A recipe for "Easy Weeknight Shakshuka," opened in February, scrolled past 2,000 words of a woman's childhood memories, never cooked.
- Three separate tabs of the same article titled "How to Stop Opening So Many Tabs."
- A YouTube video paused at 0:04 since August.
- A pair of hiking boots in a cart that has been "just about to check out" for fourteen months.
- A bank login page that had timed out so long ago it had developed its own ecosystem.

At 11:40 a.m., a volunteer located a tab from March that was still playing audio. The source of the audio has not been identified, and officials have asked the public not to speculate.

## A growing problem

Experts say incidents like Pruitt's are increasingly common. A spokesperson for the fictional Institute of Window Management said the average adult now keeps tabs open "the way medieval people kept relics: out of fear, faith, and a vague sense that throwing them away would anger something."

"Every tab is a promise," the spokesperson said. "I will read this. I will buy this. I will become a person who makes shakshuka. Closing it means admitting you won't. Most people would rather lose the tab they need than face that."

## Status

As of press time, the search had expanded into a second window that Pruitt says he "didn't know was open," as well as an incognito window he described as "not relevant."

Pruitt has asked anyone with information to come forward, and confirmed he has opened a new tab to search for tips on finding lost tabs. He has since lost it.
