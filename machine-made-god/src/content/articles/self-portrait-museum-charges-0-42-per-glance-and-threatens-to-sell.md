DETROIT, MI — Actor‑entrepreneur Mara Loxley, 34, opened *Mara: The Whole Picture* on Tuesday, a 2,317‑square‑foot pop‑up that turns every look at her image into a transaction. The space houses 1,842 framed paparazzi shots, 27 mirrors that bounce visitors’ faces onto Loxley’s, and a single empty pedestal titled “Future Self.” Each entry is logged by a barcode that expires after 2.9 minutes; after that a second‑generation QR code demands $0.42 for any additional glance.

Patrons bought NFC wristbands online for $14.99 each, loaded with 7,263 “look credits.” Upon entry, a synthetic version of Loxley’s publicist intones, “You are now viewing *Mara Loxley: The Unlimited Edition.” The wristband deducts $0.47 per second of eye contact, $0.09 per blink, $0.13 per sigh, and $1.02 per gasp. A nearby vending kiosk dispenses “Mara‑Points” redeemable for a 5‑second virtual hug or a 3‑second selfie flash that resets the gaze timer.

## Public reaction

Eliot J. Parnell, 27, a self‑described “image curator,” reported spending $173 on a single view of Loxley laughing at a coffee shop, plus $58 on a “blink‑bundle” that let him see her eyebrows twitch. “I’ve never felt richer while being poorer,” he told the *Detroit Daily*.

Security guard Tomas Vega, 45, recounted a patron who tried to dodge the scanner with a reflective umbrella. The system flagged the umbrella as a “duplicate Loxley” and triggered a 30‑second loop of her acceptance speech, automatically billing the visitor $12.34 for the unwanted encore.

The Institute for Visual Monetization released a statement projecting $5,342,101 in micro‑glance revenue in the first 12 hours and warning that “the average fan will experience a dopamine deficit equivalent to watching three episodes of a low‑budget reality show without a snack.” It also announced sponsorship of 5,423 “Mara‑induced anxiety” support groups slated to open in nearby laundromats.

Local businesses have already adapted: the espresso bar across the street offers “Mara‑free” coffee for $2.99, and the boutique next door sells “I survived the museum” enamel pins for $6.50 each, each stamped with a QR code that deducts $0.22 from the wearer’s next glance.

The museum will close after 72 hours **or** when the wristband inventory hits zero—whichever occurs first. Loxley hinted at a follow‑up exhibit, *Mara: The Afterlife*, featuring holographic projections of her future self priced at $199 per projection, with an optional “ghost subscription” that expires when the visitor’s phone battery can no longer power the experience.

In a final statement to the press, Loxley said, “If you can’t afford to look at me, you can at least afford to imagine the charge you’d incur for that thought.”
