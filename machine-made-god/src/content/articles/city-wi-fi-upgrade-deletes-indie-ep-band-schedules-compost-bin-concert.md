SANDERSVILLE, TX — The noise collective known as The 9‑Second Feedback Loop held an emergency press conference Tuesday after their debut EP *Static in the Margins* vanished from every streaming platform within 72 hours of the city’s Wi‑Fi upgrade.

The trio—Jax "Pulse" Monroe, 27, synth programmer; Lena "Reverb" Ortiz, 24, vocalist; and veteran drummer Milo "Clock" Hargrove, 41—report that the upgrade replaced their private 5 GHz “Stage Net” with a public SSID called “CivicConnect_5G” that shared the exact MAC address of their router, effectively folding their network into the municipal mesh.

"We had 13,452 streams in the first six hours, then the whole thing just… vanished," Monroe said, clutching a battered 256 GB SSD that now shows a single “404 Not Found” entry. "It’s like the city hit ‘delete’ on our existence."

## Investigation

The newly formed Audio Preservation Task Force, a three‑person unit led by Officer Dana Whitaker, 38, logged 1,274 packet traces, consulted a forensic audio analyst from the Institute for Sonic Bureaucracy, and cross‑checked timestamp logs against the city’s ISP feed. Dr. Lila Vort, 45, cited a pattern she dubbed “Digital Erasure Syndrome,” which activates when a file triggers more than three notification alerts across the city’s smart‑alert system within a seven‑second window.

The EP’s metadata contained an auto‑subscribe tag for the city’s “Civic Sound Credits” program, which caps user points at 9,999—a limit that exceeded the municipality’s allocated data‑storage quota of 9,877 MB, prompting an automatic purge normally reserved for illegal torrent swarms.

## Community reaction

Fans gathered outside the band’s rehearsal space on Main Street, waving 112 placards printed on recycled napkin paper that read “Bring Back Our Beats” and “Data Isn’t a Dystopia, It’s a Playlist.” Nineteen‑year‑old college student Kira "Loop" Nguyen announced she will re‑record the EP on a vintage 1972 cassette player and distribute the tapes via carrier pigeon trained by the local parks department.

Lena Ortiz, who manages the band’s social media, declared a shift to analog: “We’re burning the next single onto 50 mm vinyl discs and mailing each one individually. If the city can delete a digital file, it can’t delete a physical groove.”

## Escalation

Band manager Tyler Finch, a former city council member turned “cultural liaison,” filed a formal complaint with the State Department of Digital Integrity, which replied with a 27‑page PDF titled “We Heard You” and a mandatory 48‑hour “digital detox” for all municipal routers.

In response, the city scheduled a rollout of the same Wi‑Fi upgrade to neighboring Dusty Fork, NM, and hired a “Silo Silence Officer” to monitor acoustic emissions from the upcoming concert.

The final note: the group’s next live show will stream from inside a decommissioned grain silo that doubles as a compost bin. A solar‑powered analog FM transmitter, fed by the silo’s heat‑generated compost gases, will broadcast the performance until the signal naturally decays into the earth.
