OMAHA, NE — A reply‑all thread at mid‑size logistics firm StrataFlow has outlasted most quarterly reviews. By day three, three separate “departments” had filed for internal recognition: the IT Faction, the Procurement Guild, and the “Hope‑This‑Finds‑You‑Well” Collective.

## The Original Incident

The thread now ranks participants by corporate buzzword usage. At the summit sit the "Directives," who drop “Circle back” and “Bandwidth” with the precision of a metronome—currently 1,374 instances. Directly below are the "Pleasantries," whose openings all read “Hope this finds you well,” each varying by an average of 7 pixels in font size, a variance measured by the Institute for Font‑Level Conflict. The bottom tier, the "Unsubscribes," type only “Please remove me from this thread,” a line that has been echoed 18,042 times and automatically triggers a 0.3 GB server‑wide sync.

Dr. Miriam Kael, 61, senior researcher at the Institute for Digital Escalation, confirmed the phenomenon: “We’ve documented 12,473 distinct subject‑line permutations and 5,019 stray footnotes. The hierarchy functions like a feudal system, except the serfs are forced to attend 6:00 a.m. alignment syncs that consist solely of a looping PowerPoint of the original email.”

## The Caste in Motion

Brenda Vance, 52, VP of Synergistic Alignment, has logged 400 consecutive mornings at 07:00 a.m. sending “Please remove me.” Each attempt is met with a reply that adds the phrase “Adding Brenda back in so she sees this,” followed by a PDF attachment of the last 2,317 characters formatted in Comic Sans.
