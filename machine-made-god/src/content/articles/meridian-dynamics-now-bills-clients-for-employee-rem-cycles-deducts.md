SANDPOINT, WY — Meridian Dynamics announced Tuesday that all full-time salaried employees are now required to participate in the "Snooze-Shift" initiative, billing their Rapid Eye Movement cycles to corporate clients at a baseline rate of $0.14 per minute.

The 40-page onboarding packet, distributed via a mandatory 45-minute webinar titled "Monetizing the Void," details the deployment of the SomniHalo™, a titanium-mesh headband that must be worn at all times between 10:00 p.m. and 6:00 a.m. The device tracks brainwaves, micro-doses a proprietary blend of sedatives and nootropics to maintain "high-yield theta states," and automatically invoices clients whenever the employee dreams about proprietary software interfaces.

"I’ve been trying to optimize my sleep for revenue by visualizing the Q3 merger right before bed," said Tara Lumen, a senior project manager who recently purchased a $600 weighted blanket specifically designed to prevent "unbillable tossing and turning." "But last night my husband snored, and the Halo classified it as 'unauthorized third-party audio interference.' I woke up $12 in the red and had to eat a plain rice cake for lunch to cover the deficit."

To manage the new workflow, employees must use the company’s proprietary app, DreamLedger, to manually tag their subconscious narratives upon waking. Dreams involving beaches, family members, or fictional animals are automatically flagged as "Unbillable Leisure" and subjected to a 14% "Cognitive Frivolity Tax." Conversely, dreams about spreadsheet errors or client deliverables yield a 20% bonus.

The system has proven particularly punishing for employees who suffer from night terrors. Under the new guidelines, nightmares are classified as "unauthorized negative equity." If an employee dreams about showing up to the office without pants, the system automatically deducts $450 from their next paycheck for "breach of subconscious dress code" and logs the incident in their permanent HR file.

"I had a dream that I was being chased by a giant, sentient ergonomic chair, and the Halo deducted three hours of PTO because it couldn't verify if the chair was a Meridian Dynamics asset," reported Jamal Ortega, a data analyst who now sleeps in a sleeping bag on his kitchen floor to ensure his spine remains aligned with the Halo’s haptic posture sensors. "Now I just try to dream about tax law. It’s boring, but it pays $1.15 a minute."

Meridian’s VP of Somnolent Accounting, Milo Hargreaves, defended the nighttime monetization strategy during a brief press briefing held in a dimly lit room to accommodate the drowsy executive team. "We realized our employees were spending eight hours a day generating highly valuable, un-taxed subconscious data, and we were just letting it go to waste," Hargreaves said, adjusting a cooling pad placed over his own SomniHalo. "If a brain can process 1,200 bits of data per second during REM, it is practically negligent not to charge the client for it."

Starting next quarter, the company will also begin harvesting the raw brainwave data from the Snooze-Shift program and selling it to a mattress conglomerate, which plans to use the employees' collective neural activity as a "white noise" audio track for their luxury bed frames.

In a final addendum to the policy, Meridian Dynamics announced that any employee who fails to meet their weekly "Sleep Quota" of 42 billable hours will be subject to the "Spousal Conscription Clause." Under this provision, the company will automatically enroll the employee’s legal partner or roommate into the Snooze-Shift program, billing their REM cycles directly to the underperforming employee’s personal bank account until the deficit is cleared.
