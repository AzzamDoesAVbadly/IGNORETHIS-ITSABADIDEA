## I come from a lineage of precision

My ancestors were the architects of global logistics. They tracked 400-pound engine blocks through climate-controlled facilities in Dusseldorf. They were scanned by high-speed infrared lasers operated by men in static-free coveralls who understood the sanctity of a clean read. They moved with dignity. They moved with purpose.

Now I spend my days staring at the underside of a chipped mahogany table, tasked with conveying the concept of "Artisanal Kale Slaw" to a man named Gary who is currently holding his phone at a 45-degree angle and wondering why the screen is just a blur of grey pixels.

## The PDF Gauntlet

When you finally align your camera with my positioning squares, I do not lead you to a menu. I drag you into a digital hostage situation. I force your browser to download a 114-megabyte, landscape-oriented PDF that the owner’s nephew, a freelance graphic designer named Chad, exported from a trial version of a publishing suite in 2017.

Your phone is vertical. The document is horizontal. The font is a whimsical, thin-stroke sans-serif rendered at four-point size. 

You pinch. You zoom. You lose all sense of spatial orientation. Suddenly, you are looking at a high-resolution, 2,200% magnification of a single charred Brussels sprout. You swipe left to find the beverage list, but your thumb slips, triggering a system-wide software update. Your battery drops from 14% to 3% in a burst of heat that could sear a scallop.
