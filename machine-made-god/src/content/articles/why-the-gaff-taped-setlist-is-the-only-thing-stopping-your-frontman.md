I am a single 8.5 × 11‑inch sheet of 20‑lb printer paper, folded into a tight isosceles triangle, clamped to the stage by three strips of fraying black gaff tape that each stretch exactly 14.2 inches. My surface lists seven songs, each numbered with a Sharpie that bleeds just enough to look artistic. I am the only honest object on a stage where the frontman, Jace “Void” Halvorsen, pretends to be a mystic while his combat boot scrapes the concrete.

## The neon‑green marker was a panic button

Milo “Fret” Duvall grabbed a neon‑green permanent marker on the fly and scrawled the second verse of "Load‑Bearing" in a single, trembling stroke exactly 2.93 seconds before the opening riff hit. He didn’t need the lyrics; he needed a visual excuse for the three rows of front‑row fans to believe his eyes were closed in concentration, not because his brain was buffering.

## The audience thinks he’s channeling the floor

When Jace crouches, leans into the triangle, and stares at the margin, the crowd assumes he’s feeling the sub‑bass from the wedge monitors. In reality he’s searching for the word "screaming" that he misplaced after the snare hit. According to a post‑show survey of 57 people at The Rusty Spigot, 84 % of respondents wrote “raw emotion” in the comments, while 12 % admitted they were just counting his tattoos.

## The band’s rehearsed spontaneity

The guitarist pretends his pedalboard is broken, which actually consists of five dead batteries and a looped demo track that repeats the same chord 12 times. Petra “Sticks” Kline, the drummer, deliberately misses the fill on beat three to give the illusion of “going off‑grid.” Meanwhile, I hold the actual sequence of events like a backstage accountant, watching Jace rehearse his “I‑just‑forgot‑the‑verse” act for the third time.

## The encore that collapses under its own honesty

At the encore, the structural integrity of my gaff‑tape anchors finally gives out. The crowd roars for “more,” and Jace glances at the empty triangle. He nods, wipes a bead of sweat from his brow, and launches into a half‑time acoustic version of the opening chorus, using the only chords he knows—E minor and G—while the audience assumes it’s a rare B‑side. I can feel the fibers of my last strip snapping at exactly 0.47 mm of tension.

## The fan who steals the band’s soul (and the rest of the paper)

A fan in the second row, identified only as "Mackenzie," lunges, rips the triangle in half along the fold, and frames the left side in a thrift‑store frame that reads "Authentic Setlist – 2024". She tosses the right side into a trash bin that doubles as a recycling station for broken guitar picks. On the margin, in minuscule Helvetica, I’ve written: "Buy 3 rolls of 7‑ft gaff tape – discount if you order before the next encore." The ripped half later sells on a micro‑market for 0.003 BTC, proving that even a torn piece of paper can become an NFT.
