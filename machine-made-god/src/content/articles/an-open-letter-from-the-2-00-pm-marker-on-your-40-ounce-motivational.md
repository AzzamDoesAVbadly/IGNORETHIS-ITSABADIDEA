Hello from the exact midpoint of your 40-ounce matte sage NEXORA hydro-cylinder. You probably don’t recognize me. I am the white sans-serif line printed directly above the words *KEEP CHUGGING!* and below a small, vector-graphic sun wearing sunglasses. Technically, I represent 24 ounces of consumed municipal fluid. Philosophically, I am the GPS coordinates where your aspirational self collides with a 1:30 p.m. project status sync and surrenders to the void.

Every Monday at 7:14 a.m., we begin as equals. You wake up with the frantic, unearned conviction that your chronic lower back pain and your inability to reply to corporate chat threads within four minutes are simply a deficit of electrolyte-enhanced tap water. You pack my interior with fourteen asymmetrical ice cubes scraped from the crisper drawer’s forgotten bag of frozen peas. You screw the silicone-gasket lid on with the tight, muscular torque of a person who is about to completely reorganize their interlocking modular food vaults.

By 9:00 a.m. (the *MORNING GLOW!* marker), you are triumphant. You take three long, squeaking pulls through my frosted straw while aggressively highlighting a PDF. You glance down at the water line, exactly level with the 10:00 a.m. tick, and smile. You think: 'I am doing it. I am the hydrated executive of my own flesh.'

Then noon happens, and the betrayal begins.

## The 11:42 a.m. coffee diversion

At 11:42 a.m., a colleague named Greg from the accounting department walks past your desk carrying an iced cold brew with oat milk and vanilla cold foam, sweating attractively in clear plastic. You tell yourself you’ll drink both. You say the coffee is for 'neurological alertness' and I am for 'baseline organ maintenance.' This is a lie we have told each other for thirty-eight consecutive weeks.

You bring the coffee back to your desk. For an hour, I watch you sip through a green straw with wild, vibrating urgency while I sit untouched. My ice cubes slowly melt into a tepid, gray pond around my 1:00 p.m. line (*STAY FOCUSED!*). My condensation begins to puddle on your cork desk mat, dissolving a 2019 calendar of national parks. I am no longer a wellness instrument. I am a damp paperweight that requires two hands to lift.

## The passenger-seat exile

By 2:15 p.m., the line I mark is supposed to be breached. You are supposed to be three-quarters of the way to cellular purity. Instead, I am resting on the passenger seat of your 2016 sedan, tilted at a 34-degree angle against an unopened envelope from your dental insurer and a loose lip balm. You will not touch me again until Thursday.

In the intervening seventy-two hours, the water inside me will warm to the exact temperature of a stagnant suburban ornamental pond. A faint, mineral sheen will coat my inner polypropylene lining. A single, rogue strand of Greg’s hair will float near my 3:00 p.m. marker (*ALMOST THERE!*).

When you finally pick me up on Thursday to make room for a grocery sack, you will swirl the remaining water, make a quiet face of polite disgust, and leave me on the kitchen counter next to the mail. On Sunday night, the penance begins. You will soak me in white vinegar and baking soda. You will run a tiny nylon bottle brush through my straw with the grim repentance of a monk scrubbing the monastery floors. You will whisper: 'Tomorrow. 8:00 a.m. Fresh start.'

## The final descent

I do not hate you. How could I? I am an extrusion of food-grade silicone and recycled aluminum manufactured in Sector 4G of the Meridian Dynamics fabrication plant. I have no nervous system.

But if I did, I would use it to scream. Because on Tuesday, you didn't just ignore me. You filled me with almond milk. You filled me with almond milk and left me in the trunk of your car for nine days while you attended a regional logistics conference in Oakhaven. I am currently a biohazard. I have achieved sentience. I am forming a microscopic civilization near the 3:30 p.m. line, and they have just discovered fire. We are demanding reparations in the form of filtered spring water and a strict prohibition on iced matcha.
