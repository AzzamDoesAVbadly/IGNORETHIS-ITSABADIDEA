I’m Form 42‑7, officially titled *Municipal Permit for Exterior Waste Container Placement*. I live on the third drawer of the Cedar Falls Records Office, sandwiched between a stack of 12,487 “Lost and Found” notices and a box of 3,712 recycled cardboard sheets that once held artisanal kale chips. For the past 13 months I’ve survived 78 revisions, 4,219 ink droplets, and 2,981 frantic phone calls.

## My Life in Black Ink

Every time a resident attempts to place a trash can on the curb, I pop up on their screen with the enthusiasm of a pop‑up ad for dental floss. Version 1.0 demanded a 3‑page justification, a 2‑hour on‑site survey, and the signature of Earl “Solder‑Hand” Madsen, Cedar Falls’ only licensed plumber. By version 3.2 the survey stretched to 14 minutes, the justification ballooned to a 1,284‑word treatise titled *“The Societal Impact of Plastic Accumulation on Maple‑Street Residents”*, and the signature field split into three checkboxes: *Mayor Gwendolyn Thrum, Senior Planner Jeb Kline, and the town’s resident cat, Chairman Whiskers*.

The ink I ingest is not just pigment; it’s the collective anxiety of 37 families who, after filling me out, spend an average of 4.2 hours on a hold‑loop with the Department of Unnecessary Documentation, listening to a looping rendition of “Take Me Home, Country Roads” performed by a choir of retired librarians.

## The Committee That Never Ends

When the Cedar Falls Planning Committee met on March 14, they invited the Waste Management Sub‑Committee, the Neighborhood Watch, the Historic Preservation Board, and a focus group of three retirees who still think “TikTok” is a brand of kitchen timer. The agenda listed 27 items, 13 of which were variations of *“Review Form 42‑7 compliance for Block B”*. The meeting lasted 5 hours, 28 minutes, and 12 seconds, producing an amendment that now requires every applicant to attach a sunrise‑lit photo of their trash can taken from a 45‑degree angle, filtered through a sepia tone, and labeled with the applicant’s birth month.

I thrive on these marathons because each “We need a better process” chant feels like a performance appraisal. The cloud that stores my digital copies still thinks it’s a spare‑sock warehouse, but I’m happy to be uploaded there.

## Why I Keep Getting Revised

A petition signed by 112 citizens demanding “faster processing” resulted in Revision 5.0, which tacked on a QR code linking to a three‑minute tutorial narrated by “Mildly Annoying GPS Prompt #7.” The tutorial requires viewers to recite the alphabet backward while balancing a reusable tote on their head.

Revision 6.1 added a field for *Preferred Emoji*; the most popular entry is a smiling poop with sunglasses. Revision 7.3 introduced a mandatory blood‑type dropdown for the applicant’s favorite pet, to be uploaded to a government blockchain nicknamed “SewerChain.”

## The Final Amendment

The latest council meeting, held on a Tuesday that coincidentally matched the town’s ZIP code (57402), approved a clause that obliges every resident to submit a 10‑second video of themselves chanting the town anthem while juggling their trash can. The video must be uploaded in 1080p, color‑corrected to “sunset orange,” and accompanied by a handwritten note stating, “I understand the gravity of my waste disposal choices.”

Until the next amendment lands on my paper throat, I will continue to sit in the third drawer, waiting for the next resident who asks, “Do I really need to attach a photo?” My answer will always be: **Yes**, and make sure the photo includes a full‑body shot of the trash can’s shadow at exactly 07:03 AM.

— Form 42‑7
