BURLINGTON, VT — A precision swatting motion directed at a kitchen fruit fly on Tuesday has resulted in the total algorithmic restructuring of local copywriter Dennis Farber’s digital identity, transforming the 34-year-old into the internet’s most aggressively pursued consumer of Scandinavian timber processing infrastructure.

According to Farber, the shift occurred at 7:42 p.m. while he was scrolling through a short-form video feed. A 6-second clip of an automated softwood debarking drum appeared on his screen just as a winged insect landed on the bezel. Farber’s thumb froze above the glass for approximately 2.4 seconds as he lined up a strike with a rolled-up utility bill.

"I didn’t even see the log," Farber said, while wearing a pair of industrial-grade anti-fog safety goggles he accidentally purchased via one-click checkout at 1:14 a.m. "I just wanted the fly dead. When I looked back down, my entire feed was a series of whitepapers on bark-to-core volumetric ratios in the Dalarna province."

Within ninety minutes, Farber’s digital ecosystem had purged all traces of his actual interests—mid-century furniture and amateur birdwatching—replacing them with a high-density stream of heavy machinery operating in sub-zero conditions. By midnight, the profile the internet had built for Farber included:

- Three separate newsletters delivering real-time spot-market price alerts for kiln-dried spruce pulp.
- A series of 40-minute, silent drone tours of gravel haul routes outside Sundsvall.
- A targeted ad for a 40-ton pneumatic harvester offering "zero down for licensed Nordic haulers through October."
- An automated calendar invite for a virtual roundtable on the 'Structural Integrity of Cross-Laminated Timber in High-Wind Coastal Zones.'

"He was supposed to be picking out a pasta sauce on the grocery app," said Farber’s partner, Chloe Vance. "By ten o'clock, he was staring into the middle distance, muttering about sap viscosity and asking if our rental duplex had a 480-volt three-phase electrical hookup for a commercial band saw."

Farber attempted to reset the system by manually searching for "funny golden retriever videos," but the feed returned a 14-minute training module on using Karelian Bear Dogs to secure remote logging perimeters from aggressive woodland fauna. By Thursday morning, he had received six LinkedIn connection requests from regional sales directors at Finnish hydraulic valve conglomerates and a pre-approved $4.2 million commercial loan from a bank in Stockholm.

At press time, Farber was thirty minutes into a certified European forestry management webinar, taking frantic notes on root-collar preservation and wondering if his current wardrobe was "too casual" for a meeting regarding the export of 12,000 metric tons of unpeeled birch across the Baltic Sea.
